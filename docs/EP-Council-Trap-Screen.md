# EP-Council — Trap Screen T0–T12

The Council Trap Screen is run by the Green Team after **every red-team round**. Any trap firing halts progress until the issue is addressed.

Traps are named after the supermajor whose institutional failure most clearly exemplifies the risk.

## T0 — Strategic Drift

**The non-negotiable stop.** T0 fires whenever the opportunity contradicts the Strategy Brief on any of the 7 Strategy Brief dimensions: geography, asset type, capital envelope, return thresholds, portfolio philosophy, financing posture, or mandate constraints.

**What happens when T0 fires:**
1. The council halts immediately
2. The contradiction is documented explicitly
3. The user must choose: (a) reject the opportunity, or (b) formally amend the Strategy Brief before proceeding
4. If the Strategy Brief is amended, Phase 0 re-locks before any further assessment

T0 cannot be overridden by strategic rationale, timing pressure, or council consensus. It is a structural check, not a vote.

## T9 — Acquisition Leverage Trap (New in v9)

**The Occidental lesson.** T9 fires when an acquisition is financed with debt that cannot be serviced from the target's own cash flows at $30/bbl for 24 consecutive months without an equity raise or dividend suspension.

**The $30/bbl / 24-month test:**

> *Can the target's standalone FCF at $30/bbl service the acquisition debt for 24 consecutive months?*
> *Yes → proceed to further analysis.*
> *No → T9 fires. No synergy premium, no integration argument, no strategic rationale substitutes for this test.*

**What T9 does NOT prohibit:**
- Leveraged acquisitions where the target's own FCF passes the $30/bbl / 24-month test
- A second acquisition after the first acquisition debt is substantially retired
- CrownRock-style re-entry (Occidental, December 2023) — a chastened acquirer with substantially retired debt, targeting a standalone tier-1 asset

**Why the Chevron walkaway price matters:**
If a competing bidder walked away at a lower price, that price is a data point. The premium paid above the walkaway is not proof of quality — it is evidence of price discipline failure. Occidental paid $38B when Chevron walked at $33B. The $5B premium nearly destroyed the company.

**Scale adjustment:** The T9 test applies at any scale. The absolute numbers differ; the logic is identical. One tier-1 asset at low leverage beats two tier-2 assets at high leverage in every downturn.

## T10 — Duration Drift (New in v9.1 / Skill v1.7)

**The Shell lesson.** T10 fires when a major funds distributions before/instead of the best organic reinvestment, allows R/P (reserve life) to shorten materially faster than production, or uses M&A as the primary reserve-replacement mechanism without a working organic engine.

**The core distinction:** *acquired duration ≠ created duration.* A buyback flatters EPS for a quarter; a producing barrel underwrites the next decade. When the shell of distribution discipline replaces the substance of organic renewal, the portfolio drifts from compounder to harvest vehicle without anyone naming the shift.

**What T10 watches for:**
- Distribution policy fixed first, then organic capex sized to whatever remains
- R/P shortening materially faster than production (i.e. reserves consumed faster than replaced)
- M&A as the primary reserve-replacement mechanism — acquiring barrels because the organic engine isn't producing them

**What T10 does NOT prohibit:**
- High distributions where organic reinvestment is fully funded first
- M&A to add tier-1 acreage alongside a functioning organic program
- Buyback programs in genuine harvest-vehicle strategies that have been explicitly chosen (rare, and must be in the Strategy Brief)

**Relationship to T4 (Complexity Creep):** Both are Shell-named, both are portfolio-shape traps. T4 catches *additive* drift (segments added without removal). T10 catches *subtractive* drift (organic renewal starved to fund distribution). A major can pass T4 and still fail T10.

## T11 — Megaproject Overrun (New in v9.2 / Skill v1.8)

**The Chevron lesson (Gorgon).** T11 fires when a megaproject FID's economics are only demonstrated at the sanction-case cost and schedule, with no viability test at a P90 overrun.

**The evidence base (four supermajor case studies):**

| Project | Operator/venture | Sanctioned | Delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gorgon LNG | Chevron | ~$37B | ~$54B (+46%) |
| Tengiz FGP | TCO (Chevron-led) | $37B | ~$48.5B (+31%), startup slipped to 2025 |
| Kashagan | NCOC consortium (ENI early-phase operator) | — | ~$116B cited total; a decade late |
| Mozambique LNG | TotalEnergies | ~$20B | +$4.5B and +5 years from a single security event |

**The P90 test:**

> *Does the project still clear the return threshold at P90 cost and schedule — not the sanction case?*
> *Yes → proceed to further analysis.*
> *No → T11 fires. Peer-best execution is not a plan; it is an assumption wearing a plan's clothes.*

**Scope:** development FIDs above ~$5B gross capex or ~3-year build. Short-cycle drilling programs are exempt (their overrun risk is self-correcting per well); acquisitions are covered by T9, not T11.

**Relationship to T2 (Tier-2 Masquerade):** Both are Chevron-named. T2 catches buying the wrong asset; T11 catches building the right asset wrongly. Gorgon is the proof that tier-1 resource + disciplined sanction can still produce tier-2 returns through execution — discipline has two halves: what you buy, and how you build.

## T12 — Peak-Price Entry (New in v9.3 / Skill v1.9)

**The ExxonMobil lesson (XTO).** T12 fires when an acquisition or asset-entry price clears the return hurdle only at (or above) commodity prices prevailing at announcement — with no valuation test at normalized mid-cycle prices.

**Why T12 exists when T2, T6 and T9 already do:** an all-equity, tier-1, on-thesis acquisition at peak prices passes T2 (asset quality), T6 (strategy fit) and T9 (no debt) — and still destroys value. McKinsey analysis cited by CrudeTruth found more than 50% of upstream deals above $1B since 2011 failed to create shareholder value, with commodity-price timing at entry the single most reliable predictor of failure. T9 tests the debt; T12 tests the price.

**The evidence base:**

| Deal | Entry conditions | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| ExxonMobil–XTO (2010) | ~$41B at the top of the US gas cycle | US gas assets written down ~$17–20B (2020) |
| BHP US shale (2011) | $20.6B at ~$120/bbl oil | $13B pre-tax write-downs; sold 7 years later for $10.8B after ~$15B further capex — CEO: "the timing was way off" |
| Berkshire–ConocoPhillips (2008) | Stock bought at ~$140/bbl oil | Buffett's self-described major error — same failure at any level of sophistication |
| Shell–BG (2015–16) | $3.5B projected synergies assumed ~$90/bbl | Oil at ~$30 by the Feb 2016 close; synergy cash value collapsed while the price stayed fixed |

**The normalized-price test:**

> *Is the price paid conservative relative to normalized mid-cycle earnings power across the full cycle — not the strip on announcement day?*
> *Yes → proceed to further analysis.*
> *No → T12 fires. High prices at entry are the most reliable predictor of acquisition failure; a peak is not a plateau.*

**Three-variable check (CrudeTruth framework):** value creation requires all three simultaneously — (1) entry price at/below mid-cycle, (2) asset breakeven that survives trough (~$35/bbl survives everything; ~$65 is peak-only), (3) conservative capital structure. Occidental–Anadarko survived T12+T9 exposure only because sub-$40 Permian breakevens held — variable 2 rescued variables 1 and 3, which is luck, not design.

**What T12 does NOT prohibit:**
- Counter-cyclical or mid-cycle entries priced on normalized earnings (Concho 2020, Noble 2020)
- Peak-period deals paid in stock at like-for-like peak valuations, where relative value is preserved and the balance sheet is untouched — but the normalized-price test must still be shown
- Building instead of buying: construction cost against calculable capacity (e.g. Golden Pass LNG) is the structural alternative to paying a premium on someone else's reserve estimate at spot prices

**Relationship to neighbours:** T2 catches the wrong asset; T11 catches building it wrongly; T9 catches financing it fatally; T12 catches paying for the cycle top. A deal must clear all four.

## Running the Trap Screen

The Green Team runs the trap screen after every red-team round. The screen covers all 13 traps in sequence.

For each trap:
1. State whether it fires: **CLEAR** or **ACTIVE**
2. If **ACTIVE**: document the specific evidence and halt until addressed

A round cannot close with any trap in **ACTIVE** status unless the full council votes to acknowledge and explicitly accept the risk — and the Enforcer documents the override.
